I wasn't looking forward to writing this post, but I know it has to be done if I'm being honest about this year's experience. 

Every year the Fair is truly a snapshot of life in Minnesota, and this year's Fair reflected a state that's still trying to process the trauma we experienced this winter. You could feel it in the art and see it in the faces of the people who lingered over these pieces longer than others, solemn and deep in thought. Art is a form of resistance and healing, and Minnesota turned to art to try to navigate through an incredibly tough year.

I'm not going to have any commentary about these pieces, except to add context where it might not be apparent to non-locals. 

You can click on the pictures to see a bigger version, which might be necessary to see some of the details in the pieces.

_Author's Note: Some readers might interpret this as a political post. **It is not**. It is a post about a community trying to recover from trauma through art. Regardless of the politics that precipitated the events depicted here, to interpret this as a political post would be to dismiss the humanity of a community in pain and verified truths about what happened here. Please don't do that._

_The Rebel Loon depicted in some of the pieces became a widespread symbol of resistance. It was created by Moorhead resident Bernardo Anderson as a mashup of the Star Wars rebel alliance logo and our state bird. You can read more about its origin in [this Star Tribune article](https://www.startribune.com/how-the-rebel-loon-became-a-symbol-of-ice-resistance/601588374)._
